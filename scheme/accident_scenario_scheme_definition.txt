SCHEMA DEFINITION

The output must strictly follow the provided JSON schema. Each field has a specific meaning and must be filled according to the rules below.

General Rules

Evidence

The "evidence" field must contain a short quote copied directly from the source that supports the extracted value.

If source = "incident_description":
The evidence must be copied verbatim from the incident report text.

Do not invent, paraphrase, or infer evidence.

If no supporting evidence can be found, set:
evidence = "N/A"
source = "Not specified".

Source

The "source" field indicates where the information was obtained, the allowed source are listed in each nodes.

Allowed values:

incident_description  
Information directly extracted from the incident report.


Not specified  
Used when the information cannot be determined from the incident report or reliable external knowledge sources.

Node ID

Each node must include a `node_id`, which serves as a unique identifier in the graph.

The `node_id` must follow the prefix corresponding to the node type:

- H#  → HazardConsequence nodes
- En# → Entity nodes
- C#  → Condition nodes
- Ev# → Event nodes

Where # is a sequential integer starting from 1 within each node category.

Examples:
H1, En1, C1, Ev1

Rules:
- Each node must have exactly one `node_id`.
- All `node_id` values must be unique within the output.
- The prefix must match the node's `node_type`.
- Do not invent other ID formats.
- The `node_id` is only an identifier and must not contain semantic meaning.

--------------------------------------------------
Material
--------------------------------------------------

Definition
The hazardous substance involved in the accident scenario.

This refers to the chemical or material present in the process that participates in the release, dispersion, ignition, or hazard consequence.


--------------------------------------------------
Location
--------------------------------------------------

Definition
The physical place or equipment where the accident-related event occurs.

This may include process units, storage tanks, pipelines, packaging areas, or other facility locations relevant to the accident.

--------------------------------------------------
IntermediateEvent
--------------------------------------------------

An intermediate event that occurs after the initiating event and before the final hazard consequence, 
only when a necessary accident-process step cannot be adequately represented by the existing node types.

Use IntermediateEvent sparingly.

Do not create an IntermediateEvent if the same accident logic can already be represented by:
- MechanicalFailure
- InitiatingEvent
- IgnitionSource
- Entity nodes
- Condition nodes

IntermediateEvent should only be used for a process-significant transition that adds unique causal 
information and cannot be expressed by the other node types.
Do not use IntermediateEvent merely to restate an observed outcome such as overpressure, explosion, 
or fire unless that stage is distinctly necessary in the accident progression.
--------------------------------------------------
Phase
--------------------------------------------------

Definition
The physical phase of the material at the time of the accident.

This condition describes whether the material exists as liquid, vapor, solid, or a two-phase mixture, which can influence release behavior and hazard consequences.


--------------------------------------------------
Temperature
--------------------------------------------------

Definition
The temperature condition of the material or environment during the accident scenario.

Temperature can affect vapor generation, chemical stability, and the likelihood of ignition or hazardous reactions.

--------------------------------------------------
Pressure
--------------------------------------------------

Definition
The pressure condition of the material or process system during the accident scenario.

Pressure influences the containment, release rate, and phase behavior of the material.
Elevated pressure can increase the driving force for a release and accelerate discharge from equipment, while low or atmospheric pressure conditions may affect vapor formation and dispersion behavior.

--------------------------------------------------
Volatility
--------------------------------------------------

Definition
The tendency of a substance to vaporize.

High volatility substances can easily form flammable or toxic vapor clouds that may contribute to explosion or dispersion hazards.


--------------------------------------------------
Combustibility
--------------------------------------------------

Definition
Whether the material is capable of burning in the presence of an ignition source.

Combustible materials can participate in fires or explosions when mixed with air and exposed to ignition.


--------------------------------------------------
ToxicSubstance
--------------------------------------------------

Definition
Whether the material has toxic properties that can cause harm to humans or the environment.

Toxic substances may produce hazardous exposure through vapor dispersion or direct contact.


--------------------------------------------------
Asphyxiant
--------------------------------------------------

Definition
Whether the substance can cause oxygen displacement or interfere with oxygen transport in the body.

Asphyxiants may lead to suffocation or oxygen deficiency hazards in confined or poorly ventilated spaces.


--------------------------------------------------
Conductivity
--------------------------------------------------

Definition
The electrical conductivity property of the material.

Low conductivity liquids may accumulate static electricity during transfer operations, increasing the risk of static discharge ignition.


--------------------------------------------------
ReleaseType
--------------------------------------------------

Definition
The manner in which hazardous material is released into the environment.

This condition distinguishes between instantaneous releases and continuous releases, which influence dispersion behavior and accident escalation.


--------------------------------------------------
IgnitionSource
--------------------------------------------------

Definition
The source of energy that can ignite a flammable mixture.

Examples include static electricity sparks, open flames, hot surfaces, electrical equipment, or mechanical sparks.


--------------------------------------------------
Confinement
--------------------------------------------------

Definition
The degree to which flammable or hazardous material is enclosed within a confined space.

High confinement can increase explosion overpressure and significantly influence explosion severity.


--------------------------------------------------
Dispersion
--------------------------------------------------

Definition
The spreading of released material into the surrounding environment.

Dispersion determines how vapors, gases, or toxic substances distribute in air and whether hazardous concentrations can form.


--------------------------------------------------
MechanicalFailure
--------------------------------------------------

Definition
A physical failure of equipment that contributes to the accident.

This may include rupture, leakage, seal failure, structural failure, refractory degradation, 
or malfunction of process equipment that leads to loss of containment or abnormal process behavior.

If the report describes a plausible or observed physical failure in the analysis section, 
extract it as a MechanicalFailure even if the report does not confirm it as the exact root cause.

If a mechanical failure clearly occurred and contributed to the accident,
but the exact failed component or exact failure mode cannot be determined
from the incident description, use "Unknown mechanical failure" as the name.

Use "Not specified" only when the incident description does not provide enough
support to identify whether a mechanical failure should be represented at all.

--------------------------------------------------
InitiatingEvent
--------------------------------------------------

The first abnormal operational or process event that initiates the accident sequence within the process system.

Prefer events that represent a process upset or system disturbance.

Do NOT use an observed consequence (e.g., overpressure, explosion, fire) as the InitiatingEvent 
if it can be explained as the result of other identified causes (e.g., mechanical failure or process conditions).

If a concrete MechanicalFailure is identified, it should generally precede or explain the InitiatingEvent, 
rather than being replaced by it.

--------------------------------------------------
IgnitionSource Special Rule
--------------------------------------------------

If ignition clearly occurred in the incident, but the exact ignition source
cannot be determined from the incident description, use
"Unknown ignition source" as the name.

Use "Not specified" only when the incident description does not provide enough
support to identify whether an ignition source should be represented at all.

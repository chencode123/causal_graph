Negative Example

Condition: Vapor

Incorrectly listed precursor CONDITION:
    - Chemical combustible
        - Incorrect reasoning:
            “The vapor must be of a combustible fuel to be flammable and relevant to a VCE.”
        - Why this is inappropriate:
            Chemical combustibility is not a causal precursor for vapor formation.
            Vapor forms due to phase change, evaporation, or aerosolization,
            not because of flammability.

Condition: Delayed Ignition

Incorrectly listed precursor CONDITIONS:

    1. Pre-mixture
        - Incorrect reasoning:
            “A fuel/air mixture had to be present prior to ignition, implying a time delay.”
        - Why this is incorrect:
            A flammable mixture is required for any ignition, but this does not imply formation
            as a ‘pre-mixture’ at the release point.
            Delayed ignition may occur after gradual dispersion rather than instantaneous mixing.
            Therefore, Pre-mixture is not a causal precursor for delayed ignition.

    2. Vapor
        - Incorrect reasoning:
            “Fuel in the vapor phase had to be present in air before ignition could occur.”
        - Why this is incorrect:
            Vapor is necessary for forming a vapor cloud, but vapor alone is not a direct precursor
            to delayed ignition.
            The causal condition is the formation of a flammable vapor cloud, not merely the existence of vapor.
            Thus, Vapor is not an appropriate causal precursor.

Correct precursor CONDITION that should have been identified:
    - Vapor cloud formation
        - Why:
            A flammable vapor cloud must accumulate and travel before reaching an ignition source,
            which creates the time delay between release and ignition.
            This is the true causal precursor for delayed ignition.
